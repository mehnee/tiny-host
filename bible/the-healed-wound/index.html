Revelation 13 · the order of the events

The Healed Wound

One of the beast's heads is struck dead, the wound is healed, the world wonders, and only then is authority given for forty-two months. That order is not decoration — it dates the whole chapter. Whatever healed the wound must stand before the months begin, and the months are the 1260 years of papal supremacy, 538 to 1798.

And I saw one of his heads as it were wounded to death; and his deadly wound was healed: and all the world wondered after the beast.Revelation 13:3

First

The chapter gives its own order

Verses 1 to 5 run as a chain of past-tense verbs, each one following the last. Read them in order and the sequence is not a list of themes but a history: a wound, a healing, the world's wonder, worship, and then a grant of time.

  1. "I saw a beast rise up out of the sea, having seven heads and ten horns"

    θηρίον ἀναβαῖνον — thērion anabainon, "a beast coming up"

    Revelation 13:1

  2. "the dragon gave him his power, and his seat, and great authority"

    ἔδωκεν αὐτῷ ὁ δράκων — edōken autō ho drakōn, "the dragon gave to him"

    Revelation 13:2

  3. "one of his heads as it were wounded to death"

    μίαν τῶν κεφαλῶν ὡς ἐσφαγμένην εἰς θάνατον — mian tōn kephalōn hōs esphagmenēn eis thanaton, "one of the heads as slain unto death"

    Revelation 13:3a

  4. "and his deadly wound was healed"

    ἐθεραπεύθη — etherapeuthē, "was healed"

    Revelation 13:3b

  5. "and all the world wondered after the beast"

    ἐθαύμασεν ὅλη ἡ γῆ — ethaumasen holē hē gē, "the whole earth marvelled"

    Revelation 13:3c

  6. "they worshipped the dragon… and they worshipped the beast"

    προσεκύνησαν — prosekynēsan, "they worshipped"

    Revelation 13:4

  7. "power was given unto him to continue forty and two months"

    ἐδόθη αὐτῷ ἐξουσία ποιῆσαι μῆνας τεσσαράκοντα δύο — edothē autō exousia poiēsai mēnas tessarakonta dyo, "authority was given him to act forty-two months"

    Revelation 13:5

The rule this yields

The healing is step four; the forty-two months are step seven. The wonder and the worship stand between them as the effect of the healing and the reason for the grant. So the wound must be healed before the months begin. Any reading that heals the wound after them has the chapter running backwards.

Second

The dates that fit the order

On the day-for-a-year reading, forty-two months of thirty days is 1260 years. The Reformers' sequence reads the chapter as one continuous history of Rome, and every station has a date.

  1. 476

    The deadly wound

    "one of his heads as it were wounded to death"

    The last emperor of the West is deposed. The imperial head — a form of Roman government, not a man — ceases to exist.

    Revelation 13:3a

  2. 538

    The wound healed, and the grant of time

    "and his deadly wound was healed… power was given unto him to continue forty and two months"

    Rome rules again from the same city, under a bishop who carries the old imperial title Pontifex Maximus. The healing and the start of the 1260 years meet here, in the order the text requires: healed first, then the authority.

    Revelation 13:3b, 13:5; Daniel 7:25

    538 → 1798 · the forty-two months, 1260 years

  3. 1517

    The angel with the everlasting gospel

    "having the everlasting gospel to preach unto them that dwell on the earth"

    Inside the 1260 years, not after them — the witness against the power, while the power still holds.

    Revelation 14:6

  4. 1798

    The dominion taken away

    "they shall take away his dominion, to consume and to destroy it unto the end"

    Berthier takes Pius VI from Rome and he dies in exile. This ends the months. It is not a second wound: what falls is the dominion, not the head.

    Daniel 7:26

  5. 1929

    Not the healing

    "the beast that was, and is not, and yet is"

    The Lateran Treaty restores a sovereign Vatican, and the recovery since is real. But it comes 131 years after the authority it would have to precede, so it cannot be the healing of verse 3. It belongs to the beast's "yet is" phase.

    Revelation 17:8

A head was wounded, not the beast

John is precise: μίαν τῶν κεφαλῶν (mian tōn kephalōn, "one of the heads"). The beast lives on through the wound of one of its heads, which is why a form of government can end while the power continues. Revelation says the heads are successive:

And there are seven kings: five are fallen, and one is, and the other is not yet come… And the beast that was, and is not, even he is the eighth, and is of the seven.Revelation 17:10-11

"Of the seven" — the eighth is not a new beast but the old one in another form. That is what a healed head means: the same power, governing again under a different head.

"As it were wounded" does not mean the death was avoided

The objection is that ὡς ἐσφαγμένην (hōs esphagmenēn, "as slain") only describes an appearance. But John uses the same construction of Christ: ἀρνίον… ὡς ἐσφαγμένον (arnion… hōs esphagmenon) — "a Lamb as it had been slain" (Revelation 5:6). The Lamb's death was not hypothetical. The word marks how the thing appeared in the vision, not that the slaying was unreal — and the shared vocabulary is exactly what makes the healing a counterfeit of the resurrection.

The second beast can only come after the healing

The beast from the earth is defined by what it makes men do, and the object of that worship is described by its scar:

And he exerciseth all the power of the first beast before him, and causeth the earth and them which dwell therein to worship the first beast, whose deadly wound was healed.Revelation 13:12

A power whose whole work is to point men to an already-healed beast cannot be at work before the healing. Its documented marks fall centuries later still — the crusades from 1095, transubstantiation made dogma at the Fourth Lateran Council in 1215, the Inquisition from 1231, the Index in 1559 — and the image it commissions is made to a beast "which had the wound by a sword, and did live" (Revelation 13:14).

Where the creation week puts them

Revelation names three places a beast comes up out of, all with the same participle ἀναβαῖνον (anabainon, "coming up"). Two of them are days of the creation week; the third is what was there before the first day.

On the reading of a creation day as a thousand years, counted from the fall at about 3970 BC, Day 5 runs from AD 31 to about 1031 and Day 6 from about 1031 to 2031. The sequence above then falls out on its own: rising, wound and healing are all complete by 538, with five centuries of Day 5 still to run, and the second beast's work belongs to Day 6 — which is also the day man is made in God's image, and the day Revelation has men making an image to the beast instead.

What the day fixes, and what it does not

A day names when a creature is brought forth, not how long it lives. The fish of Day 5 do not vanish on Day 6, and the beast's 1260 years likewise run on across the boundary. Read as containers the days fail; read as births they hold.

Who read it this way before

The sea-and-earth pairing on days five and six is not new, though it is rare. A search of historicist exposition from 1550 to 1950, the millennial-week literature ancient and modern, academic scholarship, seven other languages and the grey literature turned up one writer who set the two beasts in those two millennia.

And in the fifth millennium, life appeared bringing forth abundantly among the nations; and at same time, the great leviathan, the antichristian kingdom, was the peculiar work of this millennium… In the [thirteenth] chapter of Revelation, we have the two appearances of the antichristian kingdom, represented, first, as the great beast from the sea, which we have already seen on the preceding day; and a second beast from the earth.James Morison of Perth, An Introductory Key to the First Four Books of Moses, 1810

Morison died in 1809; the book was published the next year, and the passage was reprinted by Thomas Whowell in 1830 and quoted by name by G. H. Wood in 1831. He also read the two beasts as this project does — not two powers but "the two appearances of the antichristian kingdom", Rome, with an "antichristian clergy" inside it. J. P. Lange's commentary (1874) made the same day-five and day-six assignment while refusing any chronology, and Hugo Cotro's dissertation (Andrews University, 2015) established the link on the Greek Old Testament's own wording. Nobody else found so far joins the order to the dates.
